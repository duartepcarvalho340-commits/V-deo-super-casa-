HOT. 🔥 The Fed's favorite inflation gauge came in above forecasts.

October PCE (released today):
• Core PCE: 3.3% year over year vs. 3.2% expected (+0.3% on the month)
• Headline PCE: 3.8%, in line with forecasts (+0.3% on the month)

PCE in plain English: how much prices rose on everything Americans spend money on. Core strips out food and energy to show the trend underneath. The Fed's target is 2%, and inflation is moving away from it.

The other big number: Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualized (from 3.4%; ~3.5% expected). Consumer spending: 4.2%.

Strong growth + sticky inflation = little reason for the Fed to ease up. A December 9 hike was already priced in; now it looks locked in.

The long-term lens: one month is one data point. Over decades, company earnings have tended to keep up with inflation better than fixed payments. Rates move valuations now; your plan runs 10+ years.
