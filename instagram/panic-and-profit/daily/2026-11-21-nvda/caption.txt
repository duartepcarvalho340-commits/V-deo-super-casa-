Is Nvidia stock cheap now, or a value trap? Swipe for both sides 👉

Friday: -14% to $125.53, its worst day since January 2025, after Reuters reported that the SEC is examining Nvidia's accounting (no charges; the SEC declined to comment). That's ~-48% from the Oct 6 record, ~$2.7 trillion of market cap gone, and 7 straight losing weeks.

The print didn't break:
• Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8B (+103% y/y), data center $107.6B
• Non-GAAP EPS $2.68 (+111% y/y) vs. $2.47 consensus
• Q4 guide $130B ±3%, assuming $0 China data-center compute

The multiple did. Annualize Q3 EPS ($10.72) and Nvidia trades at ~11.7x, below the S&P 500's ~17x forward P/E. Implied PEG ~0.11. Earnings yield ~8.5%.

The bull case: hypergrowth at a value multiple.
The bear case: a low P/E on peak earnings is the classic semis trap. And if regulators question the E, the P/E means nothing. Add a ~340 bps margin step-down, capex funded by thinning free cash flow and no permanent CEO.

The debate isn't the P. It's the durability (and credibility) of the E.
