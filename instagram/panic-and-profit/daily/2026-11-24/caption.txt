A 10-year US Treasury now pays almost 5%, backed by the US government. So why take the risk of owning stocks? 🤔

Here's the math. Flip the P/E and you get the earnings yield: the profit you buy with every $100.

• S&P 500: ~17.5x next year's profits → earnings yield ~5.7%
• 10-year Treasury: ~4.9%
• The extra pay for owning stocks: less than 1 point. That's thin.

But a bond's payment is fixed. Company profits can grow. That's the long-term edge.

Since 1928, US stocks returned about 10% a year on average (dividends included). 10-year Treasuries: about 4.5% (Damodaran, NYU Stern).

Stocks pay more over time because they can hurt more along the way. Like last week's -8.6%.
