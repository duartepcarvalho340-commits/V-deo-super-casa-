Wall Street's fear gauge just hit its highest close since April 2025. 😱

On Friday, the VIX closed at 35.89, after touching 40 during the day.

What is the VIX? Think of it as the price of crash insurance: the market's expected S&P 500 swings over the next 30 days, priced from options. When investors panic, they pay up to protect their portfolios, and the VIX jumps.

How to read it:
• Below 20: calm
• Above 30: fear
• Above 40: panic

The twist: since 1998, when the VIX crossed 35, the S&P 500 was up about 28% a year later, on average (RBC; Cboe finds ~28% since 1990).

Not every time. In 2008, fear spiked and stocks kept falling for months. Averages are not guarantees.

But fear is usually loudest near the bottom, not the top. That's when panic selling does the most damage to long-term investors.
